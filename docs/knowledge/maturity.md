# AI SDLC maturity assessment

Source: the Plain Concepts AI SDLC Maturity Framework supplied by the presenter on 2026-10-05.
The framework belongs to the Principal Engineers and DevEx Chapter.
It assesses practices within an account vertical.

## Levels

| Level | Name | Practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | No AI | Development, documentation, review, and delivery are manual. |
| 2 | Exploratory | Individuals use AI without common standards or visibility. |
| 3 | Structured | Approved tools, guidance, and review standards exist. Adoption still varies across teams. |
| 4 | Scaled | Teams apply common standards, onboarding, metrics, and review cycles. |
| 5 | Governed & Optimized | Workflows enforce governance and quality. Measured outcomes drive improvement, with clear ownership. |

New tools do not establish a higher level.
The assessment measures how teams use them.
Do not assign Platform a score without completed ratings.

## Areas and scoring

| Area | Weight | Covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SDLC Coverage | 35% | Backlog, planning, coding, review, testing, docs, infrastructure, quality, and incidents. |
| Workflow Determinism | 25% | Reusable assets, versioning, defined outputs, and validation. |
| Control, Security & Risks | 20% | Acceptable use, information sharing, third-party tools, and cost control. |
| Measurement & Impact | 20% | Visibility, outcomes, tooling, and review. |

The framework rates 21 dimensions from 1 to 5.
Each area has a weighted mean, and the final score combines the area weights.
The bands are below 1.5, 1.5 to below 3, 3 to below 4, 4 to below 4.5, and 4.5 or above.

## Limits

The supplied page also contains figures about shadow AI, quality variance, and productivity gains.
Their primary sources are absent from the supplied text.
The keynote omits these figures.
The final world shows how the four Platform pillars support assessment practices, without claiming a measured level.
